# End-to-End Logic Review (Directive 6.3, Section A)

Complete logic audit of the platform as built through Directive 6.2, executed
before the HR-validated workflow simplification was implemented. Every case
journey (resignation with notice, immediate resignation, restricted
termination) was traced from intake to completion through the actual code
paths - services, state machines, workflow definitions, sweeps, UI surfaces -
looking for logic defects, unnecessary steps, duplicated inputs, approvals
that add no value, poor defaults, technical terminology, automation
opportunities, access-model problems, notification problems, UX friction and
reporting gaps.

Product direction for the fixes comes from HR OPERATIONAL FEEDBACK gathered
in the Directive 6.2 live-data UAT (Part 19 questions); it is recorded as
product direction, not formal policy or legal approval.

## Classification key

| Classification | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| LOGIC_DEFECT | The code does something objectively wrong or inconsistent with its own rules. |
| UNNECESSARY_STEP | A step that consumes HR effort without adding control or evidence. |
| DUPLICATE_INPUT | HR must state a fact the platform already holds or already asked for. |
| UNNECESSARY_APPROVAL | An approval that adds no decision value over the accountable operator. |
| POOR_DEFAULT | A default that forces users to discover the correct combination manually. |
| POOR_TERMINOLOGY | Technical vocabulary presented to business users. |
| AUTOMATION_OPPORTUNITY | Deterministic work performed manually. |
| ACCESS_MODEL_PROBLEM | The access model demands more accounts/logins/surface than the work needs. |
| NOTIFICATION_PROBLEM | Notification lifecycle or evidence handling is wrong or incomplete. |
| UX_FRICTION | Capability exists but the interface makes it unreachable or laborious. |
| REPORTING_GAP | Supported information is not surfaced where decisions are made. |

Dispositions: **FIXED** (in Directive 6.3) or **DEFERRED** (with reason).
Impact: HIGH (control/evidence risk), MEDIUM (meaningful daily cost), LOW
(minor cost, no control risk).

## Findings

| ID | Classification | Finding | Impact | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LR-001 | UNNECESSARY_APPROVAL | The Action Form required line-manager approval (`PENDING_MANAGER`, task `MGR_ACTION_FORM_APPROVE`) before HR final approval. HR feedback: the manager contributes no information the form does not already carry; the approval only delayed completion and forced a manager login for a purely administrative record. | HIGH | FIXED - manager approval removed from the form state machine (`packages/domain/src/actionForm.ts`); the assigned HR Officer completes ordinary forms; `HR_REVIEWED` is an optional configuration-gated oversight step (`actionForm.requireHrReview`). Legacy statuses remain renderable forever and legacy live rows flow forward without being stranded. |
| LR-002 | ACCESS_MODEL_PROBLEM | Routine IT and Finance participation required application logins, department queues, claiming, and task-surface training - for work whose real-world shape is "receive the instruction, do the work in your own systems, confirm". Two whole roles existed to press Complete on tasks that mirror an email. | HIGH | FIXED - IT_RESPONDER / FINANCE_RESPONDER are retired as operational logins (historical read support only). Departments are reached by controlled, versioned email with secure acknowledgement links (`packages/domain/src/emailActions.ts`, `packages/db/src/services/ackTokenService.ts`); v2 workflow definitions carry no IT/FINANCE login tasks; clearance judges email-action evidence instead (`packages/domain/src/clearance.ts`). |
| LR-003 | DUPLICATE_INPUT | Sagicor/NCB applicability was stated repeatedly: dedicated workflow tasks (`HR_SAGICOR_APPLICABILITY`, `HR_NCB_APPLICABILITY`), a free-text applicability note at notification preparation (K.9), and again implicitly at dispatch. The same benefit fact was re-entered in different shapes with no single source of truth. | MEDIUM | FIXED - benefits applicability lives once on the case as first-class `YES`/`NO`/`UNKNOWN` fields (`groupHealthApplicability`, `groupLifeApplicability`, `otherProviderApplicability`); HR confirms once under Benefits & providers and the rules engine derives everything downstream (`deriveRequiredActions`, `confirmBenefits`). |
| LR-004 | POOR_DEFAULT | Provider applicability had no first-class "not yet known" value: the absence of a notification record was ambiguous between "does not apply" and "nobody decided yet", so a missed provider removal was indistinguishable from a correct non-action. | MEDIUM | FIXED - `UNKNOWN` is the explicit default; it opens a `BENEFITS_APPLICABILITY_UNKNOWN` exception and blocks v2 clearance until resolved (uncertainty is open work). `YES` prepares the provider notification with provenance; `NO` records the decision and creates nothing. |
| LR-005 | AUTOMATION_OPPORTUNITY | Notifications were 100% manually prepared: HR had to know which notifications each case needed, prepare each from a template, submit, obtain approval and run the manual-send flow - purely deterministic work driven by facts the platform already held. | HIGH | FIXED - the deterministic rules engine (`packages/domain/src/derivedActions.ts`, `derivedActionService.ts`) derives every required action with provenance (rule key, rules version, source fields, result) and prepares notifications automatically under send control levels (`AUTO_SEND` / `HR_REVIEW_THEN_SEND` / `HR_MANAGER_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`); routine internal emails need zero HR clicks; HR intervenes only through exceptions. |
| LR-006 | LOGIC_DEFECT | Approving a notification with no usable recipient silently skipped dispatch: the approve transition resolved a recipient, found none, enqueued nothing, and left the record APPROVED with no signal to anyone. The notification looked handled and was never going anywhere. | HIGH | FIXED - a missing recipient is a `RECIPIENT_MISSING` HIGH exception with an actionable resolution path: HR enters a recipient for THIS case (encrypted, masked, audited `NOTIFICATION_RECIPIENT_SET`) or saves an approved recipient for future cases via the directory lifecycle; the display state `RECIPIENT_REQUIRED` is explicit (`emailActionDisplayState`). |
| LR-007 | LOGIC_DEFECT | The clearance gate accepted ONE case-wide approved `ASSET_UNRESOLVED` exception as covering EVERY unresolved asset on the case - approving an exception for a lost cable also cleared an unreturned laptop. | HIGH | FIXED - exception coverage is per item: `assetsSatisfyClearance` requires each UNRESOLVED item's id to appear in the approved-exception coverage (`approvedUnresolvedAssetIds`), and clearance reports exactly which items remain uncovered. |
| LR-008 | LOGIC_DEFECT | The `FORM_SUPERSEDED` audit event type was declared in the catalogue but never emitted: superseding a finalized Action Form on a material case correction recorded only the attestation invalidation, leaving a gap in the form's audit trail. | MEDIUM | FIXED - `outdateFormsForCaseChange` now emits `FORM_SUPERSEDED` alongside `FORM_APPROVAL_INVALIDATED` (`actionFormService.ts`). |
| LR-009 | LOGIC_DEFECT | Integration-test fixtures used fixed calendar instants; real time eventually overtook them, silently flipping test semantics - "completed early" scenarios became "completed late" without any test changing, so assertions drifted from what they claimed to prove. | MEDIUM | FIXED - fixture dates are dynamic anchors derived from the test run's clock (`packages/db/test-int/helpers.ts`: receipt ~25 days back, effective separation 5 days ahead, always future). Rule recorded in `TestStrategy.md`: no fixed calendar dates in fixtures. |
| LR-010 | LOGIC_DEFECT | The worker heartbeat logged a warning whenever ANY feature flag was enabled - but enabled automation flags are the intended operating state of the redesigned platform, so the "warning" was a guaranteed false alarm that would train operators to ignore the log. | LOW | FIXED - the heartbeat reports enabled flags as information (count and keys); production live-send safety remains with the fail-closed configuration guards and pilot activation gates, not a log line (`apps/worker/src/index.ts`). |
| LR-011 | REPORTING_GAP | The dashboard's "Also needs review" list was synthesized from assorted queue queries (returned documents, render failures, due survey invitations) instead of the exception feed - the platform's actual record of what needs attention - so exception types with no bespoke query never surfaced. | MEDIUM | FIXED - the attention surface is fed from the exception feed, which the Directive 6.3 exception types (`RECIPIENT_MISSING`, `BENEFITS_APPLICABILITY_UNKNOWN`, `ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_OVERDUE`, `AUTO_SEND_BLOCKED`) flow into with per-type deep links; new exception types appear without dashboard changes. |
| LR-012 | POOR_TERMINOLOGY | HR-facing surfaces spoke engineering: "Outbox", "Responder", raw enum values (`PENDING_APPROVAL`, `READY_FOR_MANUAL_SEND`), and decision-register identifiers (DEC-042) appeared in daily-work screens. | MEDIUM | FIXED - business-language label maps are the only vocabulary on HR-facing surfaces (`FORM_STATUS_LABELS`, `NOTIFICATION_STATUS_LABELS`, `EMAIL_ACTION_STATE_LABELS`, `REQUIRED_ACTION_STATUS_LABELS`: "Needs your review", "Scheduled", "Could not send", "Sent manually"); raw states and governance identifiers are confined to administration and audit surfaces. |
| LR-013 | UNNECESSARY_STEP | Every case presented the same generic 11-type asset dropdown, obliging HR to consider categories the employee never held; the checklist reflected the catalogue, not the person. | MEDIUM | FIXED - the asset list derives strictly from the employee's issued assets (`RULE_ASSETS_FROM_ISSUED_V1`; one recovery action per issued item, "No company assets are recorded as issued" when none); adding an unlisted asset is the explicit exception path (ADD UNLISTED ASSET), not the default posture. |
| LR-014 | POOR_DEFAULT | For immediate resignations the employee is usually gone before an acknowledgement can be obtained, yet the form defaulted to PENDING and HR had to discover the valid `NOT_OBTAINED` + reason combination by trial and error on every such case. | MEDIUM | FIXED - `suggestedAcknowledgement` pre-populates NOT_OBTAINED with the standard reason for immediate resignations (and NOT_APPLICABLE for terminations) at form creation; HR reviews rather than discovers. |
| LR-015 | UX_FRICTION | The task service accepted `evidenceAttachmentId` on completion, but no UI ever supplied it - the evidence parameter was dead weight and task evidence stayed in free-text notes. | LOW | NOTED - with departments acting by email, completion evidence now flows through the email-action confirmations (secure-link redemption, HR-recorded evidence with channel and note) rather than task attachments; the task-level parameter remains for historical rows and future HR-task use. |
| LR-016 | REPORTING_GAP | The reporting services support cohort and location filters that the reports UI never exposed, so HR could not segment where segmentation was already implemented. | LOW | DEFERRED - reporting scope is unchanged in Directive 6.3 (the directive is workflow simplification); the filters remain supported at the service layer and surfacing them is a small, isolated later change. |
| LR-017 | UX_FRICTION | Termination survey eligibility requires an explicit HR-Manager override (DEC-014 discipline), but the web UI never exposed an override surface - the capability existed only at the service layer. | LOW | NOTED/DEFERRED - the derivation rule handles the override where recorded (`RULE_SURVEY_ELIGIBILITY_V1` reads the override on the invitation); a dedicated override surface awaits the DEC-014 policy decision rather than pre-empting it. |
| LR-018 | AUTOMATION_OPPORTUNITY | Exit-survey scheduling was a manual HR task (v1 `HR_SURVEY_INVITE`) even though the schedule rule (day 3 / day 7, DEC-013) is configuration - HR re-derived a fixed rule by hand on every eligible case. | MEDIUM | FIXED - the worker schedules due survey invitations automatically under the standard automation gates (`scheduleDueSurveyInvitations`, sweep step 9); the survey never blocks clearance (unchanged fixed default). |
| LR-019 | NOTIFICATION_PROBLEM | Departmental notifications had no receipt or completion evidence at all: `MANUALLY_SENT` was terminal, so "we emailed IT" was the strongest statement the platform could make about access removal - delivery was silently treated as done. | HIGH | FIXED - the evidence chain `SENT → ACKNOWLEDGED → CONFIRMED_COMPLETE` with acknowledgement tokens, HR-recorded evidence (email/phone/other with note), `ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_OVERDUE` escalation (CRITICAL for IT at the effective instant), and the clearance rule that email delivery is never proof access was removed (U.8). |
| LR-020 | UNNECESSARY_APPROVAL | Approval authority for notifications keyed on recipient class alone (K.2/K.3), so every derived internal department email would have demanded the same human approval path as an external letter - re-imposing the manual workload the redesign removes. | MEDIUM | FIXED - derived records follow their snapshotted send control level: `HR_REVIEW_THEN_SEND` is ordinary HR case work (`notification.approveInternal`); only `HR_MANAGER_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` demands management authority; restricted cases always resolve to the stricter of the configured levels. |
| LR-021 | POOR_DEFAULT | Confidential-timing sends had no mechanism: nothing could hold a termination notification until the effective instant, so correct timing depended entirely on a human remembering not to send early. | MEDIUM | FIXED - `scheduledSendAt` is first-class: termination IT emails release no earlier than one hour before the effective instant, termination Finance emails at the effective instant (`itEmailScheduledAt`, `RULE_FINANCE_EMAIL_V1`), and the outbox honours the schedule on both automatic and manual approval paths. |
| LR-022 | NOTIFICATION_PROBLEM | Notification templates existed only as seeded rows with a read-only listing; HR could not draft, preview, test or activate a template version, so a missing or faulty template was unfixable without engineering. | LOW | FIXED - versioned notification template management (`notificationTemplateService.ts`): officers draft (plain text, allow-listed placeholders, no markup), preview/test-send use synthetic sample values and the development capture only, HR Manager activates (prior ACTIVE atomically superseded), with full audit (`TEMPLATE_NOTIFICATION_*`). |

## Method note

Findings were verified against the working tree (module paths cited per
finding), the Directive 6.2 operational-effectiveness feedback and the
seeded v1 workflow definitions. No finding was closed on the strength of a
UI change alone; each FIXED disposition names the enforcing domain or
service code. The redesign that implements the FIXED dispositions is
recorded in `HRValidatedWorkflowRedesign.md` and the build record in
`Directive63BuildRecord.md`.
